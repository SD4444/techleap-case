Clean weeding, bankable demand 01 / 15

Cleaning the water table through the removal of herbicides

A demand-side intervention to stimulate the uptake of herbicide alternatives in farming.

A water board as anchor payer. Growers signing conditional orders. Techleap structuring the commitment so investors can underwrite it.

The challenge

Clean water and effective weed control have to coexist.

Water authority

Has a legal duty to protect surface-water quality. A few named herbicides keep monitoring sites above their standard. The decline in exceedances is too slow for the 2027 objectives.

Grower

Pays for a weed-free field, on time, at a margin. Herbicides treat a whole field cheaply. An alternative that misses weeds costs yield this season and adds weed seed for later seasons.

Two linked market failures. The grower pays for weed control and society pays for the water pollution. The robot maker needs production capital to serve buyers who will not commit until the technology is proven at scale.

Evidence of demand

Authorities already pay to reduce pollution. Growers already test alternatives.

National policy

2030 vision, 2027 deadline

Future Vision for Crop Protection 2030: avoid products where possible, near-zero emissions. Water Framework Directive objectives due 2027.

Water board

Funds farm measures, within limits

Rijnland: more than 30 listed measures at 40%. Mechanical weeding equipment capped at €10,000 per applicant. One application at a time, not an order book.

Growers

Reported need, trials under way

Sectors report that losing benfluralin made early weed control in inulin chicory uneconomic; herbs and witlof lack approved products and labour. Public trials compare electric, mechanical and spot-spray machines.

Expressions of interest

Named growers who say they would buy. No price, volume or delivery window. No acceptance tests. Nothing enforceable. Investors do not commit.

Signed conditional purchase Proposed

Identified buyer with a contractual obligation. Crop, region, volume, price, window and capacity stated. Acceptance tests agreed before supplier selection. Investors still bear production risk, but demand is no longer speculative.

The intervention Proposed · working name

A Clean Weeding Purchase Commitment.

  1. Select the narrow problem

    One crop, one catchment, one herbicide application to displace.

  2. Assemble buyers and an anchor payer

    Growers commit conditional minimum volume at a defined price. The water board reserves an approved contribution toward the price difference.

  3. Pay for a real validation

    Suppliers compete for paid work on a small area with independent data.

  4. Activate the larger purchase

    Tests pass, the buyer's purchase obligation activates.

  5. Show investors the transaction

    Signed terms, creditworthiness, field results, unit cost, utilisation.

Techleap holds no public money and buys no robots. The water board's participation is a proposal until it approves a budget and a lawful instrument. Precedent: Frontier's advance market commitment for carbon removal.

Deep dives

Two routes for the public contribution

Pay per verified hectare, or fund adoption.

Preferred first design

Purchase commitment per verified hectare

The water board pays the price difference per eligible hectare actually treated and independently verified. Growers sign conditional minimum-volume orders. Open question: can the board lawfully commit across seasons, and will buyers sign meaningful volumes?

Alternative

Conditional farmer adoption facility

Grants or rebates for equipment or services that pass stated tests. A budget alone gives a supplier possible customers, not orders, so growers must also sign purchase reservations. Open question: timetable and subsidy-rule compatibility.

Deep dive

Decision gates

Seven facts to establish before execution.

  1. Crop × substance × catchment

    A named herbicide above the limit, a crop using it, a robot that displaces that application.

  2. Real farmer economics

    Invoices, hours, rescue treatments, willingness to sign at a price.

  3. Technology readiness

    Field records for efficacy, passes, uptime, cost. Not marketing.

  4. Contract strength

    Enforceable volume, price, remedies. Two investors confirm it changes underwriting.

  5. Water-board anchor

    Funding route, duration, state-aid check. Without it the order must work privately.

  6. Novelty

    Beyond what rental, leasing and existing subsidies already provide.

  7. Scope fit

    Deep-tech commercialisation remit first. No emissions claim without lifecycle analysis.

Techleap's role

Assemble buyers, structure the commitment, bring in investors.

Buyers sign

A grower group and an anchor payer commit conditional volume, price and acceptance tests for one crop in one catchment.

Validation passes

Independent field data on weed control, yield, uptime, cost and displaced chemical use.

Investors underwrite

At least two investors confirm the signed order book changes their production-round terms.
