Idea 01

Investor Requirements Library

Collect recurring investor requirements and turn them into practical, sector-specific guidance for startups.

Modality · Asynchronous

Investor requirements are collected, structured and made available for startups to use independently.

The problem

Startups often discover what investors require only after fundraising begins. The requirements are fragmented across investors, sectors and individual financing processes.

How it works
CollectStructureApplyUpdate
  1. 1 · Collect

    Interview investors, production financiers and specialist lenders. Record the evidence they require and the recurring reasons investment decisions are delayed or declined.

  2. 2 · Structure

    Organise the findings by sector, company maturity, capital need, intended use of funds and financing type.

  3. 3 · Apply

    Turn the findings into evidence checklists, sector guidance and standard templates that startups can use before approaching investors.

  4. 4 · Update

    Test the guidance against live financing cases and revise it when investor requirements change.

What startups receive
  • Sector-specific investor guidance
  • A tailored evidence checklist
  • A standard format for presenting the financing case
  • Clearer feedback on missing evidence
What Techleap learns

The same information reveals recurring financing barriers across companies and sectors. Techleap can use these patterns to improve its programmes and identify where wider intervention may be needed.

This is not an investor directory, matchmaking platform or promise of capital. It helps startups understand what investors need to make a decision.

Idea 02

Sector Capital Working Groups

Bring investors and startups in the same sector together to explain what they need, what they can offer and what prevents them from moving forward.

Modality · Live and participatory

This addresses the same information gap through direct discussion. Investors and startups explain their needs, constraints and points of disagreement to one another.

The problem

Startups do not always understand which investor requirements are essential, which are negotiable and which reflect the constraints of a particular fund.

Investors may not fully understand the development timelines, capital requirements and commercial realities facing companies in the sector.

Both sides make decisions with an incomplete view of the other.

Startups
  • Capital needs
  • Available evidence
  • Commercial constraints
  • Fundraising headwinds
Facilitated sector discussion
  • Compare assumptions
  • Examine disagreements
  • Identify possible changes
Investors
  • Investment mandate
  • Evidence requirements
  • Risk constraints
  • Reasons for declining
Shared sector findings

Areas of alignment · Points of disagreement · Actions for both sides · Structural barriers

How it works
  1. 1 · Gather both perspectives

    Before each session, investors and startups provide confidential input on:

    Investors
    • What they are trying to invest in
    • What evidence they need
    • Which risks they can and cannot accept
    • Why relevant opportunities fail to progress
    Startups
    • What capital they need and when
    • What evidence they can currently provide
    • Where investor requirements are difficult to meet
    • Which financing conditions prevent them from progressing
  2. 2 · Hold a structured discussion

    A neutral facilitator brings a small, relevant group together around the areas where their responses differ.

    The discussion uses real, anonymised financing cases to examine:

    • Where expectations are aligned
    • Where each side misunderstands the other
    • What startups could change
    • What investors could change
    • Which barriers neither side can solve alone
  3. 3 · Publish the findings

    Produce a concise, anonymised sector brief covering:

    • Investor requirements
    • Startup financing needs
    • Recurring points of disagreement
    • Practical actions for both sides
    • Structural barriers requiring further intervention
Delivery

Run recurring working groups for sectors such as climate hardware, biotechnology, agricultural technology and advanced manufacturing.

Publish the findings on a shared platform so other investors and startups can use them. Update each sector brief as market conditions and investment behaviour change.

The benefit

Startups gain a clearer view of how investors make decisions. Investors gain a better understanding of the constraints facing companies in the sector. Techleap can identify where better information is sufficient and where a wider market intervention may be required.

This is not a networking event, pitch competition or investor panel. It is a structured discussion designed to improve understanding on both sides of the market.

How the two ideas connect
02 · Sector Capital Working Groups01 · Investor Requirements Library

The working groups can generate and test the findings published in the Investor Requirements Library. The library makes those findings available to startups that did not attend.

Idea 03

Buyer Signal Network

Collect structured information from potential customers about the problems they need solved, the evidence they require and the barriers that prevent them from adopting new technology.

Structured customer intelligence
The problem

Startups often rely on individual interviews, pilot interest and market forecasts to demonstrate demand.

Investors cannot always determine whether this represents a real customer need, while information about purchasing requirements remains fragmented across potential buyers.

How it works
  1. 1 · Define the buyer need

    Potential customers provide structured information on:

    • The problem they need solved
    • The limitations and cost of the current solution
    • Required performance
    • Acceptable economics
    • Validation or certification requirements
    • Procurement timelines
    • Internal barriers to adoption
  2. 2 · Combine related signals

    Group similar needs across customers within the same sector.

    Show which requirements are widely shared and which apply only to individual buyers.

  3. 3 · Map relevant technologies

    Startups explain how their technology addresses the need and provide the evidence currently available.

    The platform shows where a relevant solution exists and what remains unproven.

  4. 4 · Capture the buyer response

    Buyers state:

    • Whether the proposed solution addresses the problem
    • Which evidence is still missing
    • What would be required for a pilot or procurement process
    • Which commercial or operational barriers remain
What it provides
For startups
  • Problems customers are actively trying to solve
  • Evidence required for adoption
  • Purchasing and validation requirements
  • Reasons promising technologies fail to convert into sales
For investors
  • Whether several customers share the problem
  • How valuable and urgent the problem is
  • Which commercial assumptions remain unverified
  • How long adoption may take
For buyers
  • Technologies that may address their needs
  • How other buyers define the same problem
  • Shared validation or procurement barriers
  • Opportunities to coordinate industry requirements
The benefit

Startups gain clearer evidence of what customers need. Investors receive a stronger basis for assessing commercial demand. Buyers gain visibility into relevant technologies and the barriers they share with other organisations.

This is not an advance market commitment.

Buyers do not reserve funding, guarantee purchases or endorse individual suppliers. The system captures demand and adoption requirements. Any later procurement programme or purchase commitment would be developed separately.
